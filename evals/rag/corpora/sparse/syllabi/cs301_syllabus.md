# CS 301 syllabus

## Description

CS 301 Database Systems, Fall 2026, 3 credits, Prof. Alicia Chen. Prerequisite: CS 201 Data Structures. The course covers how to design, query and tune relational databases.
By the end of CS 301 students will be able to: (1) model a domain with an ER diagram, (2) turn it into a normalized (3NF) relational schema, (3) write correct SQL with joins, subqueries, aggregation and window functions, (4) use indexes and query plans to explain performance, and (5) explain transactions, ACID and concurrency control.

## Materials

Textbook (recommended, not required): Silberschatz, Korth and Sudarshan, *Database System Concepts*, 7th edition. Lecture slides and the old midterm are posted on the course site.
Software: SQLite 3.39 or newer and DB Browser for SQLite (both free). Homework is submitted on Gradescope; questions go on the Ed Discussion forum.

## Weeks 1–8

- Week 1 (Aug 24): introduction, relational model, keys
- Week 2 (Aug 31): ER modeling and cardinality
- Week 3 (Sep 7, no class Mon for Labor Day): functional dependencies, normalization to 3NF
- Week 4 (Sep 14): SQL basics, NULLs, joins
- Week 5 (Sep 21): subqueries, IN vs EXISTS
- Week 6 (Sep 28): GROUP BY and HAVING; intro to indexes (Oct 2)
- Week 7 (Oct 5): relational algebra; HW3 due Fri Oct 9
- Week 8 (Oct 12): midterm review; project milestone 1 due Fri Oct 16

## Weeks 9–16

- Week 9 (Oct 19): review session Mon 18:00; midterm Wed Oct 21; window functions begin Fri
- Week 10 (Oct 26): window functions and CTEs; HW4 due Fri Oct 30
- Week 11 (Nov 2): indexes, B+ trees, EXPLAIN query plans
- Week 12 (Nov 9): query optimization; project milestone 2 due Fri Nov 13
- Week 13 (Nov 16): transactions, ACID, concurrency control and recovery
- Week 14 (Nov 23): NoSQL and distributed databases (Mon only; Thanksgiving break Nov 25–27)
- Week 15 (Nov 30): project demos in class and lab (10 minutes per team)
- Finals: cumulative final exam Thu Dec 10, 08:00–10:00

## Grades

Weights: homework 25%, labs 10% (lowest dropped), project 25%, midterm 15%, final 25%. Letter grades: A ≥ 93, A− ≥ 90, B+ ≥ 87, B ≥ 83, B− ≥ 80, C+ ≥ 77, C ≥ 73, C− ≥ 70, D ≥ 60, F < 60. Prof. Chen may lower cutoffs but never raises them.
Regrades: request through Gradescope within 7 days of grades being released, with a short explanation. The whole question may be regraded, so the score can go down as well as up.

## Attendance

Lecture attendance is not graded, but slides don't cover everything said in class. Lab attendance is required: each lab is graded on in-lab completion, and the lowest lab score is dropped. A missed lab can be made up within one week by emailing the TA, Marcus Webb (mwebb@example.edu), with a reason.

## Collaboration

Students may discuss homework ideas with classmates, but every student must write and submit their own SQL. List anyone you discussed with at the top of your file.
AI tools (ChatGPT, Copilot, local models, etc.): allowed for explaining concepts, debugging error messages and studying. **Not allowed** for generating homework answers. For the team project, AI-generated code or SQL is allowed only if it is disclosed in the project README with the tool used and what it produced. Undisclosed AI use counts as an academic integrity violation and is reported to the Dean of Students.

## Communication

Post course questions on Ed Discussion first; Prof. Chen answers email (achen@example.edu) within 2 business days. Students with accommodations should send their Disability Resource Center letter to Prof. Chen in the first 3 weeks of term, or as soon as it is issued. A missed midterm needs documentation (medical or family emergency, via the Dean of Students); with it, the final exam weight replaces the midterm. There is no makeup midterm.
