- **Instrumental view:** technology is a neutral tool; what matters is how humans use it ("guns don't kill people…").
- **Substantive view:** technology isn't neutral; it shapes how we see the world and ourselves, whatever we intend. Heidegger is the classic substantive thinker; Borgmann and Winner sit in between.
- **Anthropological definition:** technology as human activity. Heidegger later calls both definitions "correct" but not yet "true".

In the *Phaedrus*, Socrates tells the myth of Theuth offering writing to King Thamus. Thamus says writing will weaken memory and give the appearance of wisdom without the real thing. Writing is a **pharmakon**: both remedy and poison. Irony: we only know this because Plato wrote it down.

**Techne** = craft knowledge of how to make things; **episteme** = theoretical knowledge. Aristotle's four causes, using the silver chalice example that Heidegger reuses:
- material cause: the silver
- formal cause: the chalice shape
- final cause: its purpose in the ritual
- efficient cause: the silversmith
Heidegger's twist: the four causes are four ways of being *responsible* for bringing something forth, not just mechanical causes. Know this example for Essay 1's opening section.

Reading: "The Question Concerning Technology" (Lovitt translation). Main moves:
- The usual definitions (tool, human activity) are correct but miss the **essence** of technology, which is "nothing technological".
- Technology is a way of **revealing** (Greek *alētheia*): a way the world shows up for us.
- Old craft is **poiesis**, a *bringing-forth* that lets things emerge (the chalice, a windmill that uses the wind without storing it up).
- Modern technology is a **challenging-forth** (*Herausfordern*): it demands that nature supply energy that can be extracted and stored (coal mining, the hydroelectric plant on the Rhine).
Lindqvist's Rhine contrast: the river in Hölderlin's poem vs the river as a "water power supplier" or a tourist-industry object.

- **Standing-reserve** (*Bestand*): things show up only as resources on call, ready to be ordered and optimized (the airliner on the runway, the forest as paper supply). Even people: "human resources".
- **Gestell / enframing**: the *gathering* or framework that sets everything up to appear as standing-reserve. It is not a machine or a thing; it's the way of revealing that rules the modern age. That's why the essence of technology is "nothing technological".
- **The danger:** enframing hides every other way of revealing, so we stop seeing anything (including ourselves) except as resource.
- **The saving power:** quoting Hölderlin, where the danger grows the saving power grows too. Reflecting on the essence, and art (poiesis), can open another relation.
Lindqvist: enframing is not "technology is evil". It's a claim about how things are revealed, not a moral verdict.
